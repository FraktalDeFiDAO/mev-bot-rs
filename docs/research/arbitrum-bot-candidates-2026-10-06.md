# Arbitrum bot contract candidates — 2026-10-06

Six additional **historical cyclic-arbitrage executors** have successful transaction evidence below. These are contracts, not just tagged EOAs: read-only `eth_getCode(address,"latest")` on the [Arbitrum One public RPC](https://arb1.arbitrum.io/rpc) returned substantial runtime bytecode for all six on 2026-10-06. Current profitability and recent activity are **not established** for these historical examples. The [companion analysis](arbitrum-bot-analysis-2026-10-06.md) separately covers current Aave liquidators and `0x5A35e7396664B2024B29433eb189F1F72B41Fdba`.

The selected raw transactions, receipts, bytecode-size observations, and Sourcify lookup statuses are retained in [the evidence snapshot](arbitrum-bot-candidates-evidence-2026-10-06.json).

## Discovery and confidence

The authors of [*Optimistic MEV in Ethereum Layer 2s*](https://tik-db.ee.ethz.ch/file/4f1aa8a957f6ae513ff78360184a752e/) publish their [classification implementation and data](https://github.com/kingbaldwin-iv/Optimistic-MEV). Their [Arbitrum contract list](https://github.com/kingbaldwin-iv/Optimistic-MEV/blob/main/arb.parquet) contains 2,249 addresses in a `tx_to` column. The study covers August 2023–May 2025; its detector reconstructs swaps, excludes routers/aggregators, and looks for cyclic profitable flows. Dataset membership is useful discovery evidence, not proof of present activity or a source-code audit. The six selected addresses match the paper's largest Arbitrum bot table and have independently checked successful receipts.

For **every historical candidate below**, a [Sourcify chain-42161 lookup](https://sourcify.dev/server/v2/contract/42161/0x689368aae04fe7d961d1e545b28266c4f179151c?fields=all) returned HTTP 404 on the research date. Arbiscan address/code pages were blocked during retrieval. Therefore the verification classification is **“source verification unresolved; no Sourcify record found”**, not a categorical claim that the contracts are unverified on Arbiscan. Verified token/pool source shown inside an explorer transaction is not verification of the executor itself.

## Operator families and compact input

The `0x6893…151c` and `0x9e52…5867` samples both come from `0x879c2a2f7e4071ebdc971e508885d4a8cdeaf227`, both call selector `0x113452c3`, and both pay `0xeebe…968f`. This supports treating them as a shared execution family when measuring competition; it does not identify the real-world owner. Actual comparisons need equivalent routes and current L1/L2 fee accounting. ## Practical next analysis

1. Replay successful competitor routes against our venue/pool graph, especially V4↔V3, stablecoin combinations, and non-AMM pricing venues. An omitted profitable route is an actionable coverage gap; the historical payouts alone are not a current opportunity.
2. For a current executor, collect successful trades, caught-error successes, outright reverts, gas, effective priority fee, and token/inventory deltas over a defined recent window. Measure net results rather than success status or transfer count.
3. Decode compact calldata and callback structure only after identifying implementation and authorization. Benchmark identical routes before concluding compact input improves cost or latency.
4. Group contracts by shared sender/beneficiary/bytecode before ranking rivals. The six historical addresses represent fewer than six independently evidenced operators.
5. Use current ordering rules and current bids. The 2023–2025 samples predate the repository's recorded September 2026 ordering changes; their `Timeboosted: False` labels and low historical tips do not establish a current ordering advantage.

No contract transactions were sent and no bot code was changed.
